=== ADR 0004 — Efficiency is tested twice: its shape in the suite, its size in the dogfood

Date:: 2026-11-04

Status:: Accepted (PoC)

[.links]
owned-by:: <<sh-maintainer>>
orr:upholds:: <<qg-fast>>

[.triggers]
baseline:: 2026-11-04
on time.elapsed:: every P6M

==== Context

Projecting the whole log for each request is linear by design
(<<adr-0001,ADR 0001>>). The dogfood timed the view, but several costs went
unmeasured: the write path, a restart, memory, the size of each answer, and,
above all, how cost _scales_. Nothing would notice a second pass over the log
per node, per day or per reading.

Two constraints shape the answer:

* *Timings belong to a machine* (<<adr-0003,ADR 0003>>). In the suite a timing
  budget is either flaky or too loose to catch anything.
* *A probe's version pins its method, not its workload.* An edit to the demo
  changes every timing, and nothing said so.

==== Decision

===== 1. The suite pins the shape, without a clock

The suite counts and compares, and never times. It checks that:

* reads per stored event stay the same with ten times the nodes, more ribbon
  days or bigger batches;
* the universe answer keeps its size as readings grow;
* a batch at the documented limit is accepted whole, and one more is refused.

The property is that the count does not change, whatever the constant. Another
pass over the whole log is allowed. A pass per node, per day or per reading
fails.

===== 2. The dogfood measures the size, after a week of live signals

Dogfood probes time a write, a restart, the heap held and the size of the
answer. They run against a fixed workload: a week of the simulator's signals.
Every target sits well above its baseline, so a regression shows and noise
does not.

===== 3. The workload is part of the yardstick

A timing probe declares the fixed workload it measures. The suite pins the
size of each workload by its event counts, not by a hash. A reworded demo
keeps timings comparable, but a new node, signal or cadence changes the counts.
That fails the suite and names every probe to bump.

==== Consequences

* An edit to the demo that changes the workload's size means bumping every
  timing probe. That is the point: readings before and after are different
  quantities.
* Jitter, energy, cost and distribution are left out on purpose. They need a
  load test, a meter or business data, or contradict the single-process design.
* Each open console costs a full projection every 5 s, whether or not anything
  changed (<<td-projection,§11>>).

==== Revisit when

* snapshots or incremental projection land, since the pass counts then pin a
  different shape;
* the dogfood runs in CI, where heap and timings need their own environment
  name.
